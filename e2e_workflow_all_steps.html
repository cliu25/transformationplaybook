Starting point: You've been asked to transform a workflow with AI. Before touching any workflow, you need two things that make everything else possible: a senior leader who has genuinely committed to sponsoring this work, and an honest read on whether your domain can actually build something. This step is about securing both.

The single most important action you can take right now is not choosing a workflow — it's finding a real sponsor. Not someone who says "sounds great" in a meeting. Someone who will show up to a weekly 45-minute steering session, clear the procurement or access blockers that will inevitably arrive, and back this project publicly when organizational pressure pushes back. These are very different things.

Here's how to test it before you commit: ask your candidate sponsor to block the first steering meeting on their calendar before the kickoff. If they can't find 45 minutes, you don't have a sponsor yet. Keep looking. Starting without a real sponsor is the most common reason transformation projects stall — not technology, not data, not resources. Sponsorship.

⚠ Pain point
Confusing enthusiasm with commitment
A leader who endorses your idea in a meeting has given you nothing you can build on. Endorsement says 'I think this is a good idea.' Commitment says 'I will act when action is needed.' Test commitment before you start, not after you're stuck.

Ready to Move On?

  • ☐ Executive sponsor named and tested — weekly steering is on the calendar before kickoff
  • ☐ Domain focal named — one person, not a committee, accountable for day-to-day work
  • ☐ Readiness check complete — flags documented and understood
✓

Ready to advance to Step 2

Sponsor committed, domain focal named, readiness flags documented, steering cadence set. Now find the right workflow to transform.

Map your domain's workflows, understand what people actually do and what it costs, and select the one workflow most worth transforming first.

Ready to Move On?

  • ☐ Frontline conversations complete — at least 4–6 people, notes in their own words
  • ☐ Prioritization matrix complete — every candidate scored on the same four dimensions
  • ☐ One workflow selected with a written rationale; everything else explicitly parked or dropped
✓

Ready to advance to Step 3

Ranked list complete, one selection made, sponsor endorsement in hand. Entering Discover with evidence, not assumptions.

Validate that the workflow you selected is actually worth transforming.

Ready to Move On?

  • ☐ Go/no-go recommendation presented to and decided by the steering committee
  • ☐ If go: baseline KPIs confirmed with finance; tech architect has cleared data access and integration path
  • ☐ If no-go: rationale documented, next workflow selected from the prioritized list
✓

Ready to advance to Step 4

Opportunity validated with evidence. Go approved. Baseline KPIs set and finance-confirmed. The architect knows the technical landscape. Now design the solution.

Define the future-state vision, scope exactly what ships in 90 days, and produce requirements specific enough that a developer can start building.

Ready to Move On?

  • ☐ Future-state vision documented and shared with the build team
  • ☐ In-scope and out-of-scope lists both exist and have executive sponsor sign-off
  • ☐ Requirements pass the user-story test — a developer can act on them without asking questions
  • ☐ ARB review initiated — not waiting until after the sprint
✓

Ready to advance to Step 5

Vision, scope, and requirements are complete and signed off. ARB is in motion. The build team can start Day 1 without a re-explanation meeting.

Build the MVP in 90-day sprints, test it with real users doing real work, and make a data-driven go/no-go decision.

Ready to Move On?

  • ☐ Go/no-go decision made and documented — based on the criteria agreed before Day 1, not on organizational momentum
  • ☐ Decision shared with the steering committee with supporting data
  • ☐ Post-MVP backlog exists and has a named owner
✓

Ready to advance to Step 6

Go decision made and documented. Solution is live. Post-MVP backlog has an owner. Change management is ready to fully execute. Now make adoption systematic.

Get people genuinely using the solution as part of their real work, prove the value against your baselines, and hand it off with enough structure that it doesn't degrade when the transformation team steps back.

Ready to Move On?

  • ☐ Business owner and technical lead named — both have accepted accountability
  • ☐ Technical handoff complete — credentials, code, architecture, data docs, test cases transferred and verified
  • ☐ "Fully complete" defined and agreed — MECE user population, full feature set, adoption KPI target
  • ☐ Next workflow identified — returning to Step 3 with the next candidate from the prioritized list
✓

One workflow transformed. Ready for the next.

Solution owned, tracked, and adopted. Ownership transferred. Return to Step 3 — Workflow Analysis — and apply everything learned in this cycle.
