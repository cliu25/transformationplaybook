The Framework

Three phases. Six steps. One repeatable method.

The transformation unfolds in three phases — Engage, Discover, Execute — each containing two steps. The approach is explicitly repeatable: apply it workflow by workflow, domain by domain, until AI-first operations become the default.

How the steps connect

What gets built, and what gets handed off.

Steps 1–2 happen once upfront. Steps 3–6 repeat for every workflow you transform. Each step produces a concrete set of outputs that the next step depends on — nothing is thrown over the wall.

The Six Steps

What happens at each step.

For each step: what you're walking in with, what you're doing, and what you're handing to the next team. Detailed activities, artifacts, and templates live in the End-to-End Workflow.

01
Engage
Technology & Data Foundations Assessment
Timing Upfront — before committing to any workflow

Before a single workflow is analyzed, you need to know whether the domain is actually ready to build. This step is the foundation of everything: you're establishing whether the technology, data, and people needed for AI transformation exist in a usable form.

You'll assess the current state of data quality and accessibility, confirm what enterprise AI tools and platforms are available, identify integration constraints early (ARB requirements, data governance, IT dependencies), and form the project team with the right roles confirmed and committed — not just named.

Skipping or rushing this step is the single most common source of delays downstream. Teams that treat readiness as a formality often discover mid-build that their data isn't structured, their architect isn't available, or their tech stack doesn't support the approach they designed.

What you hand to Step 2
Confirmed executive sponsor and project lead
Technology & data readiness scorecard
Identified team roles (architect, product owner, change owner, value owner)
Known integration constraints and platform access
02
Engage
Business Process Mapping
Timing Upfront — runs in parallel with Step 1

With a confirmed team and a sense of technical readiness, you turn to the business. This step maps the domain's processes end-to-end — not to document the status quo, but to find where AI would create the most impact.

You'll conduct workflow interviews with domain SMEs, build a map of key processes and their interdependencies, and begin quantifying the value associated with each. The output is a long list of candidate workflows, sized by effort and potential value, with an initial hypothesis about where to focus.

This is also where stakeholder alignment begins in earnest. You're not just mapping processes — you're building shared understanding of what transformation means for this domain, and starting to get executives invested in the outcome before major commitments are made.

What you hand to Step 3
Mapped business processes with associated value estimates
Long list of candidate workflows, prioritized by impact and feasibility
Initial business case with sponsor buy-in
Aligned stakeholders and a steering committee structure
03
Discover
Workflow Analysis
Timing ~2 weeks per workflow analyzed

You walk into this step with a list of candidates. You leave with a single, committed MVP workflow and the evidence to defend that choice to anyone who asks.

For each candidate workflow, you'll go deep: map the current state step-by-step, identify where AI specifically intervenes, quantify the before/after impact, assess the data and technical feasibility, and score it against a consistent rubric. This isn't theoretical — you're talking to the people who actually do the work today.

Critically, this is also where value metrics are born. The baseline measurement that will define success at launch has to be established now, when the current-state process still exists. Waiting until after the build means you have no baseline to compare against.

What you hand to Step 4
Selected MVP workflow with scoring rationale
Current-state process map (as-is workflow in detail)
Confirmed value metrics and baseline measurements
Data and integration feasibility confirmed with architect
04
Discover
Solution Design
Timing ~2 weeks per workflow

You know the workflow. Now you design the solution. This step bridges business understanding and technical build — and it's where projects most commonly stumble. Requirements that look clear to a business analyst often turn out to be unbuildable.

The key output is build-ready requirements: specific enough that a developer can start without scheduling a clarification call. That means user personas, workflow redesign (the to-be state), data objects, system integrations, API contracts, error handling, and UAT criteria — all defined before a line of code is written.

MVP scope is also locked here. You're defining what's in 90 days and what goes on the post-launch backlog. A clear scope boundary, agreed by the executive sponsor, is what makes the 90-day sprint achievable and protects the team from scope creep.

What you hand to Step 5
Build-ready requirements package (personas, to-be workflow, data objects, integrations)
MVP scope definition with explicit out-of-scope list
Technical architecture confirmed through ARB
UAT criteria and change management plan started
05
Execute
Experimentation
Timing 90-day sprint

The build begins. Working in 2-week sprint cycles, the team develops, tests, and iterates on the MVP solution. The steering committee meets weekly — not to receive status updates, but to surface blockers, make decisions, and keep the build on track.

Value tracking starts immediately. You're comparing actual outputs against the baseline you set in Step 3, adjusting where needed, and capturing early proof points that will support the scale decision. "Red on a chart is good" — the goal of the steering committee is transparency, not theater.

De-scoping decisions will happen here. That's not failure — it's the mechanism that makes 90-day delivery possible. A well-communicated de-scope, with a clear rationale and a post-MVP roadmap for what was cut, keeps executive confidence intact.

What you hand to Step 6
Working MVP, tested and UAT-approved
Value proof points vs. baseline (quantified)
Technical handoff package: code repo, architecture, data documentation
Post-MVP backlog and scale roadmap
06
Execute
Scale & Adopt
Timing Beyond the 90-day sprint

Getting to MVP is not the end — it's the beginning of a new operating model. This step is about making the AI solution stick: deploying it into the live workflow, driving real adoption among the people who use it every day, and establishing who owns it after the project team steps back.

Ownership is the critical transition. You'll define business ownership (who manages prompts, content, and user support), technical ownership (who maintains infrastructure, models, and data pipelines), and a support model for when things break. An AI solution with no clear owner will degrade and be abandoned.

With one workflow transformed, you return to Step 3 — or even Step 2 — to select the next. The repeatable nature of this framework is what drives domain-wide transformation, not any single deployment.

What graduation looks like
Solution live in production with real users
Business and technical ownership formally transferred
Value tracking running in EBM / reporting system
Next workflow on the roadmap
